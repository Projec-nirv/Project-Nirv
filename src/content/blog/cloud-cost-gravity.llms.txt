# Revised Core Thesis
Cloud cost gravity is the loss of expected operating leverage as a software company scales. Cloud spend does not necessarily grow faster than revenue; instead, it can remain materially above forecast and decline only marginally as a share of revenue because growth-phase architecture, reliability requirements, unused capacity, commitment gaps, and fragmented ownership compound.

# Key Findings

## 1. Architecture Creates a Recurring Baseline
Microservices can improve independent deployment and team autonomy, but increase whole-system complexity. Multi-Region reliability increases infrastructure usage, operational overhead, and engineering effort. These are not automatically waste; they require explicit business justification.

## 2. Waste and Commitment Gaps Persist
- Flexera 2019: respondents estimated 27% waste; Flexera measured 35%.
- Flexera 2019: 47% of AWS users used Reserved Instances.
- Flexera 2026: estimated wasted IaaS/PaaS spend reached 29%.
- Flexera 2026: fewer than half of organizations use any single commitment-discount program across major cloud providers.
- AWS Savings Plans: up to 72% advertised savings versus eligible On-Demand usage; realized savings vary.

## 3. Governance Is Common but Outcomes Lag
- Accenture: 39% fully achieved expected cloud-cost savings.
- Flexera 2026: 71% have a cloud center of excellence or equivalent.
- Flexera 2026: 63% rely on a FinOps team.
Inference: formal governance structures are insufficient without authority, allocation data, and engineering participation.

## 4. Infrastructure Optimization Can Affect Margin
Dropbox reported gross margin increasing from 33% in 2015 to 67% in 2017, attributing the improvement to revenue growth and Infrastructure Optimization.

## 5. Valuation Effects Must Be Treated as Scenarios
A16z estimated approximately $100B in suppressed value across 50 public software companies. The estimate is retained only as back-of-the-envelope scenario analysis, not as a measured causal result.
